I’ve been thinking a lot about what it means to be a designer now that AI can actually build things.

Not generate a nice-looking screen. Actually build things.

Working software, components, design systems and prototypes. Things that previously involved several disciplines and quite a lot of handover.

That changes the job.

LedgerOS started as a way for me to explore that properly.

I wanted to see how far I could get building a fictional commercial bank, Ledger Bank, while treating AI as part of the design process rather than something I occasionally use to generate an idea or write some copy.

Commercial banking was deliberate.

Financial products are complicated. They’re regulated, need to be accessible and often have complex permissions, data and user needs. Commercial banking adds another dimension because the product has to work for very different businesses.

A small company with a couple of accounts is one thing. A large organisation with hundreds of accounts, different legal entities and users with very specific permissions is another.

I like that constraint.

If I’m going to explore what AI changes about design, I want to do it somewhere that requires systems thinking rather than somewhere I can get away with generating a few nice screens.

Pretty quickly, Ledger Bank became only part of the experiment.

The more interesting question was the system behind it.

Could I create a coded design system that an AI agent could understand and use? Could I give it components, tokens, rules and guidance, then ask it to build product experiences without explaining the design from scratch every time?

And if that worked for one banking product, what happens when I add another?

Could they share foundations? Could I change the visual theme without rebuilding either product? Could common financial patterns sit between the base design system and the individual products?

That’s where LedgerOS came from.

The name has become quite literal. I’m starting to think about it as an operating system for building financial products.

There’s a design system. There’s a financial domain. There are products such as Ledger Bank. There’s a playground for exploring the system. And now there’s this Journal.

I’m building most of it through conversations with AI agents.

I still make the decisions. Quite a lot of the work is deciding what not to build, questioning abstractions and working out where things belong. But I’m spending far less time manually producing the final artefact.

That feels like an important change in design practice.

I don’t know exactly where LedgerOS will end up yet.

That’s partly why I want to document it.

This Journal is the record of building it.
